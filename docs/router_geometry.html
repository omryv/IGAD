01

α0 measures concentration

How tightly is the routing distribution packed? Fix the normalised proportions and α0 controls the radius of the cloud, nothing else.

α0 = Σi αi E[xi] = αi / α0 → fixing αi/α0 and varying α0 changes only the tightness
In the symmetric experiment the problem is exactly one-dimensional With α = (a, …, a) we have α0 = ka, so α is a bijective function of α0. Curvature is therefore not approximately a function of concentration on this slice — it is exactly R = f(α0). Here curvature can only re-encode concentration.

Hold total concentration fixed and rotate the distribution across simplex directions. Concentration is blind by construction; curvature is not.

03

The decisive test: balanced load, hidden modes

Identical aggregate expert load, identical sufficient statistics, genuinely different token-level structure. This is where geometry would have to earn its keep.

aggregate mean = (1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4) in both conditions → load-balance loss sees no difference E[log xi] under the mixture = ¼(ψ(8)−ψ(11)) + ¾(ψ(1)−ψ(11)) = −2.280753968 best single-Dirichlet fit = α̂ = (0.549738, …), α̂0 = 2.198950, R(α̂) = 1.569331
Why R is blind here — structurally, not empirically Condition A is defined as Dir(α̂), the best single-Dirichlet fit to the mixture. The Dirichlet MLE matches E[log x], so both conditions share the same aggregate mean and the same sufficient statistic. IGAD's score is a deterministic function of α̂, and so is MLE-α0. Two populations with the same best-fit α̂ are exactly indistinguishable to either. Curvature is a scalar compression of the fit — it cannot recover what the fit discards.

The structure lives precisely in what the single-Dirichlet fit throws away.
The caveat that turned out to be the answer
A mixture of Dirichlets is not itself a Dirichlet. Because the fitted family is wrong, the scalar curvature of a single fitted Dirichlet smears out the very structure the test was built to detect.
Δ

The identifiability constraint

Why stages 2 and 3 exhaust the options for a single Dirichlet.

Within one Dirichlet family: αi = α0 · E[xi] therefore same mean + same α0 ⇒ same Dirichlet distribution verified numerically: α0 recovered from (mean₀, var₀) of Dir(1.5,4,6.5) = 12.000000000

You cannot hold both the Dirichlet mean and α0 fixed and still obtain a different single-Dirichlet shape. So within the family there are only two moves: change α0 — which stage 1 shows curvature merely re-encodes — or change direction, which stage 2 shows necessarily moves the mean and is therefore already visible to a first-moment monitor. To test whether geometry adds information you must leave the family, and stage 3 shows that when you do, curvature computed from a misspecified fit goes with it.

The proposed criterion was that if R beats MLE-α0 only when routing becomes genuinely multidimensional, that is evidence for geometry rather than concentration. Measured: R beats MLE-α0 in the anisotropic case — where mean load beats it too — and ties it at 0.39 in the multimodal case, both below chance. The criterion is met in the one place it does not discriminate, and fails in the one place it would.
